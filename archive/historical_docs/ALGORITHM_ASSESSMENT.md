# Phase 2.7 — Research and Algorithm Assessment

**Scope.** Read-only review of the Phase 1–2.6 implementation, research notes, reports, logs, checkpoints and evaluation JSON. This document is an assessment only: no model was implemented or trained, and no prior code, result, checkpoint, or test was changed. Literature and repository links were checked on 1 November 2026. Research results reported by their authors are not treated as results for this simulator.

## Executive assessment

The evidence does not indicate that PPO is simply “undertrained” or that switching to another MARL algorithm will solve the gap. The strongest explanation is a **problem-to-method mismatch**: this simulator gives each arriving task one source-owned placement decision; the action alternatives are already fully enumerated and evaluated by a model-based greedy scheduler; most transition outcomes are determined by that one decision and immediate candidate costs. Agents do not negotiate or make synchronized regional actions. In that setting, three independently trained policies add sparse data and policy-gradient variance without exploiting a meaningful multi-agent structure.

Greedy is strong because it uses the same candidate execution/carbon/latency calculations as the objective and directly picks the least-carbon feasible region (with a small latency term). It needs no value estimation or exploration to learn those calculations. It also adapts online as reservations and resource availability change. In the Phase 2.5 matched synthetic evaluation it completed every task and averaged 447.69 gCO2e; IPPO no-SPT averaged 490.38 g with 0.999 completion. In Phase 2.6's much smaller pilot, greedy averaged 314.32 g with 1.000 completion, versus 476.78 g for IPPO no-SPT and 455.62 g for MAPPO no-SPT. The latter policy mean was 21.16 g below IPPO, but uncertainty was broad, MAPPO completion was slightly lower, and its critic explained variance (-0.016) was worse than IPPO's 0.002. The pilot therefore does not demonstrate a centralized-critic learning benefit.

The most defensible next direction is **not another MARL model**. First strengthen the non-learning benchmark and measure its gap to a small-instance offline optimizer. Then, if a repeatable gap remains under variable arrivals, carbon forecasts or nontrivial contention, test a constrained hybrid: preserve feasibility and deadline protection as hard scheduling logic, and let a contextual bandit select or adjust among a small set of credible heuristic policies. Use a centralized single policy as the first learned comparator. Return to MARL only if deployment truly requires separate region controllers with coupled, simultaneous decisions.

Confidence intervals above are descriptive and rely on small synthetic samples. Phase 2.6 baselines had four workload runs; learned policies had three training seeds evaluated on four workloads. None of these results support a claim of general effectiveness.

### Likely causes, ranked

1. **Greedy has direct access to the decision model.** Candidate generation computes each feasible placement's incremental energy, emissions, runtime, migration cost and deadline feasibility. `GreedyCarbonScheduler` ranks those candidate metrics directly. The learned actor must infer a ranking already calculated by the environment, through a terminal normalized reward and a noisy value estimate. The greedy rule is close to the actual objective and is naturally sample-free.
2. **The current decision process has weak multi-agent structure.** For each task, only the source-region agent acts. Region agents do not decide concurrently over separate tasks in a synchronized team step, and destination agents do not accept or negotiate migrations. Task commitments are serialized in task-id order by the simulator. The three policies mostly partition tasks by source region; they are not a team of simultaneous decision makers in the common cooperative-MARL sense.
3. **The reward is delayed and partly clipped.** An execution yields completion plus normalized carbon and latency terms; carbon is divided by 100 g and clipped at 2, and latency is normalized by remaining deadline. Rejection is strongly penalized after Phase 2.3, but the actor receives one terminal reward for an action whose immediate carbon/latency costs are already available in its candidate features. GAE assigns delayed outcomes over deferrals; critic fit remains poor. The reward did encourage rejection before Phase 2.3, but the post-correction results show that this was not the enduring cause.
4. **There is little sequential lookahead in the current formulation.** Carbon intensity is dynamic and the candidate feature exposes intensity at expected execution start. However, the actor is not given an explicit forecast of the best future slot/candidate after deferral. Deferral advances one slot and is bounded to two attempts, after which a fixed feasibility fallback may place the task. Current tasks are independent rather than a DAG; there is no persistent task progress or multi-stage workflow to create long-horizon dependencies. Resource reservations can affect later tasks, but many small pilot tasks are feasible and the heuristic recomputes the candidates after each commitment.
5. **Actor data are small and uneven.** A regional policy gets transitions only for tasks arriving in that source region. The 18–24 task episodes and few seeds leave each actor with few samples per PPO update. PPO is on-policy, so old experience is discarded. SPT further reduced useful actor data: 37.1% overrides in Phase 2.5, 54.1% in the tiny Phase 2.6 MAPPO-with-SPT run. Phase 2.4's 32-episode sweep did not show monotonic gains.
6. **MAPPO's “global state” is only a task snapshot.** The centralized critic concatenates all three regional observations for the *same task* plus an actor identity. It does not consume a full Markov global state containing every queued task, task-specific deadline/slack distribution, all active assignments and finish times, per-region reservations, and a forecast horizon. Centralization as implemented therefore does not fully expose shared future-load dynamics to the critic.
7. **Synthetic evidence is not yet a broad scheduling benchmark.** Workloads vary resource sizes, runtimes, deadlines and arrivals, and carbon profiles are time-varying, but the distributions are hand-authored, one workload family is used in the later studies, and profiles are smooth periodic scenarios rather than measured, forecast-error-bearing grid signals. Capacity/power/network parameters are illustrative. Four held-out workloads in Phase 2.6 are especially insufficient for a generalization claim.

### Is the action space appropriate? Are agents empowered?

The four categorical logits implement `defer`, local execution and migration to either remote region, with infeasible destinations masked. This is expressive enough for the present **one-task, one-shot placement** decision. The mask is a necessary safety constraint. The action space does not directly express “wait k slots”, choose among a forecasted execution window, reserve/batch jobs, negotiate destination acceptance, or jointly allocate different simultaneous tasks. Those actions only matter if the simulator and objective make those choices part of the problem. Adding richer actions without adding corresponding realistic state and transition structure would not solve the core mismatch.

The source agent has real authority over its task's placement; the Phase 2.2 hidden carbon optimizer was removed. It does not have authority over other regions' incoming tasks or a destination's local scheduling policy, so the operational multi-agent interpretation is limited. Reward attribution is now consistent with the source agent, which is correct for the implementation but gives each regional learner a small, local dataset while shared capacity effects are global.

### Does this problem benefit from MARL?

**Not as currently formulated and evidenced.** There are three regions, but the simulator centrally has all candidate metrics and processes one task decision at a time. A single centralized dispatcher can see the same state, apply the same resource-safety checks and choose the same three destination actions. The presence of three cloud regions is not by itself a reason for three learning agents. MARL becomes defensible if the research question includes decentralized information/authority (for example, regions independently control admission and share capacity through constrained communication), meaningful simultaneous decisions, or strategic trade-offs in a genuinely coupled system. Those properties are not represented by the current source-owner protocol.

Current constraints, rewards and carbon profiles are largely known to the simulator. That makes model-based optimization and strong online heuristics unusually appropriate. Before learning, quantify how far greedy is from an offline optimum. If the gap is already small on realistic held-out traces, learning may have no worthwhile target.

## Literature and reproducible implementations

“Official implementation” below means a repository linked by the authors/project or explicitly described as the paper's implementation. A repository's existence does not imply compatibility or an easy reproduction in this Windows/PyTorch project. No repository or external dataset was cloned or installed during this assessment.

### Hybrid RL and heuristic scheduling

- **Heuristic-Guided Reinforcement Learning**, Cheng, Kolobov and Swaminathan, NeurIPS 2021. The paper develops a framework for injecting domain heuristics into RL to reduce the effective horizon and improve learning efficiency; the demonstrated settings are robotics/control and procedurally generated games, not carbon scheduling. It supports a safe hybrid idea, not a claim that this project's SPT override is optimal. The paper page/supplement was found, but an official code link for the general method was not identified in the inspected source. [NeurIPS paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/hash/70d31b87bd021441e5e6bf23eb84a306-Abstract.html)
- **Data-driven Algorithm Selection for Carbon-Aware Scheduling**, Bostandoost et al., HotCarbon 2024. FTL selects among temporal carbon-scheduling algorithms based on prior performance, predicted job length and carbon information. This is a close systems motivation for heuristic selection/meta-scheduling, though the paper focuses on delay-tolerant temporal shifting, not heterogeneous cross-region GPU placement. The paper is available; no official implementation repository was identified from the paper/author source checked. [Paper PDF](https://hotcarbon.org/assets/2024/pdf/hotcarbon24-final23.pdf)
- **GREEN: Carbon-efficient Resource Scheduling for Machine Learning Clusters**, Xu et al., USENIX NSDI 2025. A production-oriented heuristic/system design uses carbon and job efficiency while managing time flexibility; the authors report measured ML workloads and a carbon/JCT trade-off. It is an important high-quality heuristic benchmark and evidence that a carefully designed system scheduler can be strong. The official USENIX page provides paper/slides/video; it did not identify a source-code repository, so implementation availability is **not verified**. [USENIX paper page](https://www.usenix.org/conference/nsdi25/presentation/xu-kaiqiang)
- **Carbon- and Precedence-Aware Scheduling for Data Processing Clusters (PCAPS/CAP)**, Lechowicz et al., ACM SIGCOMM 2025. It adapts carbon-aware scoring to account for dependency-critical tasks, and presents CAP as a wrapper around carbon-agnostic schedulers. This supports the value of heuristics that encode structure explicitly, especially if our independent jobs become workflows. No official implementation link was found in the paper record inspected. [MIT publication record](https://dspace.mit.edu/entities/publication/8f216233-b53b-4c34-832e-638ae2e29791)
- **S.C.A.L.E: A CO2-Aware Scheduler for OpenShift at ING**, Den Toonder et al., FSE Companion 2024. This is an applied carbon-aware batch scheduler; the thesis record links a project repository. It is a useful code reference for operational heuristic design, but its workload and OpenShift deployment are not directly equivalent to this simulator. [TU Delft publication record](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid%3A79406c06-ab43-4cba-9136-cb8243e891ed) · [project repository](https://github.com/Fastjur/S.C.A.L.E)

**Suitability here:** high as a research direction if it means a constrained learned selector or small residual over known scheduling rules. Keep resource/deadline feasibility outside the learned output. Do not retain unconditional SPT replacement; use the heuristic as a baseline, safe prior, or explicit candidate. Advantage: strong prior and less data needed. Limits: policy can collapse to a heuristic, any heuristic bias is inherited, and a bandit/RL component needs honest feedback and must not bypass the safety layer. Complexity: **low–medium** for a contextual bandit selecting a small set of rules; **medium** for learned residual value over candidates.

### Value-based MARL and multi-agent Q-learning

- **The Surprising Effectiveness of PPO in Cooperative Multi-Agent Games**, Yu et al., NeurIPS Datasets and Benchmarks 2022, is the MAPPO reference implemented by the authors in the [official MAPPO repository](https://github.com/marlbenchmark/on-policy). The code supports centralized critic inputs and decentralized actors, but its documented environments are SMAC, Hanabi, MPE and Google Research Football. It provides a reproducible algorithm reference, not evidence that the present sequential placement problem requires a centralized critic. The MAPPO algorithm's repository is MIT licensed; its environment stack and training setup are much larger than the current CPU simulator. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01955)

- **QMIX: Monotonic Value Function Factorisation for Deep Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning**, Rashid et al., ICML 2018. QMIX learns local action utilities and a centralized monotonic mixing network, allowing decentralized greedy action selection under its factorization assumption. This is a credible discrete-action alternative if simultaneous cooperative agents are real. The [official PyMARL repository](https://github.com/oxwhirl/pymarl) includes QMIX, VDN, IQL and COMA in PyTorch, with Apache-2.0 code; its documented main environment is StarCraft II/SMAC, so its runtime/data/environment setup is not a drop-in fit. [PMLR paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/rashid18a.html)
- **Value-Decomposition Networks for Cooperative Multi-Agent Learning**, Sunehag et al., AAMAS 2018. VDN decomposes the team value into a sum of local Q-values. Its simple factorization is easy to interpret and can be a useful minimal control, but cannot represent arbitrary joint value interactions. Included in official [PyMARL](https://github.com/oxwhirl/pymarl). [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.05296)
- **Independent Q-learning (IQL)** is available as a PyMARL baseline. Each agent learns local Q-values without explicitly modeling other learners; this is simple, but other agents' changing policies make the transition/reward process non-stationary. In our present one-owner-at-a-time action protocol, it would be closer to three separate contextual Q-learners than a compelling cooperative MARL method. Official implementation: [PyMARL](https://github.com/oxwhirl/pymarl).
- Tan's foundational **Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning: Independent Versus Cooperative Agents** (ICML 1993) experimentally studies information/experience/policy sharing versus independent agents. It supports considering cooperation only where agents have a true joint task and useful information to share; the current scheduler's per-task ownership gives little such joint action structure. [Paper record and DOI](https://mlanthology.org/icml/1993/tan1993icml-multi/)
- **Weighted QMIX**, Rashid et al., 2020, analyzes representational limits imposed by QMIX monotonicity and changes fitting emphasis toward high-value joint actions. It is a further model choice only if QMIX's factorization is shown to be limiting; not warranted before demonstrating any need for multi-agent value factorization. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10800)

**Suitability here:** weak at present. Actions are discrete, which suits Q methods, and off-policy replay may improve sample reuse over PPO. But this implementation has no synchronized joint regional action: one owner acts on one task and receives its task-local return. A QMIX team objective would require carefully defining the timestep, inactive-agent actions, shared reward, joint action and global state, as well as credit assignment. QMIX's monotonic factorization can also fail when one region's best action depends non-monotonically on other regions' choices. Complexity: **medium-high** for IQL/DQN; **high** for correct QMIX/VDN integration and evaluation. Do not select it just because actions are discrete.

### Contextual bandits

- **Adaptive Carbon-Aware Scheduling for Federated Cloud Systems with Time-Critical Workloads**, 2026 IEEE SAMI. The paper directly compares EDF/LST plus greedy low-carbon placement with a contextual bandit over ten European clouds and time-critical workloads. It reports a bandit direction for trade-offs among carbon, deadline misses and load balance. This is directly relevant but very recent; the available IEEE record is an abstract/metadata view, so treat the paper's claims as reported rather than independently reproduced. No official implementation was identified in the official record searched. [IEEE publication record](https://doi.org/10.1109/SAMI68106.2026.11420783)

**Suitability here:** better than full episodic RL if decisions are primarily per-arrival candidate rankings and immediate action feedback is available. A linear contextual bandit (e.g. LinUCB or Thompson sampling) could select among a small menu—greedy carbon, EDF/least-slack with carbon tie-breaking, or a bounded defer rule—using task slack, candidate carbon deltas, transfer/runtime, capacities and load as context. Hard masks remain authoritative. Advantage: fast updates, simpler diagnostics, naturally uses candidate-level immediate outcome, and lower compute. Limit: contextual bandits do not model downstream capacity externalities or deferral's delayed consequence; if those effects matter, a bandit can be myopic just like greedy. Complexity: **low–medium**. This is the most defensible first learned comparator only after a better baseline suite and controlled feedback design.

For the canonical linear contextual-bandit method, LinUCB estimates action payoffs from context features and explores using confidence bounds; it provides a lightweight, interpretable starting point, not a cloud-scheduling result. [Chu et al., AISTATS 2011](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v15/chu11a.html)

### Other relevant sequential RL and optimization

- **Learning Scheduling Algorithms for Data Processing Clusters (Decima)**, Mao et al., ACM SIGCOMM 2019. Decima learns a central scheduler for stochastic arrivals and DAG workloads, using graph representations and specialized training; authors report a 25-node Spark prototype. The official project links [paper and code](https://web.mit.edu/decima/); its [repository](https://github.com/hongzimao/decima-sim) includes simulator/train/test artifacts and notes Spark integration refactoring. It is a much more structurally sequential setting than our independent tasks and has substantial code/model complexity and different dependencies. It is a credible future reference if this project adds workflow precedence, not an immediate replacement.
- **Carbon-Aware Mapping and Scheduling for Deadline-Constrained Workflows**, Schweisgut et al., 2026 preprint, and **CaWoSched**, 2025 preprint, formalize workflow scheduling with time-varying carbon and heterogeneous execution. CaWoSched combines greedy heuristics/local search and proposes an exact ILP baseline for small instances. The current project has no DAG precedence, so these are not direct algorithms to transplant; they demonstrate why an offline optimum and explicit dependency structure matter. [CWM preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27652) · [CaWoSched preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.08725)
- **A-MARL** is verified in [AMARL_VERIFICATION.md](AMARL_VERIFICATION.md): IPPO with entropy-guided SPT for a Jumanji job-shop problem, with the public [repository](https://github.com/AminAvan/a_marl-scheduler). It provides no carbon-cloud workload or objective match and is not a reason to keep MARL as the primary algorithm.
- **SustainDC** and **DCcluster-Opt** are reviewed in [IMPLEMENTATION_NOTES.md](IMPLEMENTATION_NOTES.md) and [REPRODUCTION_NOTES.md](REPRODUCTION_NOTES.md). Their datacenter-control/cluster benchmark scopes may inform future calibrated environments, but they do not establish that this three-region task placement objective benefits from multi-agent policies. No source repository or dataset should be reused without checking pinned schemas, license/terms and units.

## Recommended research direction

1. **Do not train another MARL method yet.** Preserve greedy carbon as the operational baseline and main comparator.
2. **Test whether a learnable gap exists.** Build a small exact/offline oracle for short episodes or a carefully defined relaxed MILP. Compare it with greedy on matched cases and report optimality gaps alongside carbon, energy, completion, rejection, SLA and latency. If greedy is near-optimal, terminate the RL line for this problem formulation.
3. **Strengthen heuristic controls first.** Compare current greedy, EDF/least-slack prioritized admission with carbon-aware placement, and bounded future-carbon deferral with a documented forecast horizon. Tune rule parameters only on training/validation seeds. Keep all feasibility, deadline and completion rules consistent.
4. **If non-myopic residual remains, test a centralized hybrid.** Treat each arriving task as a contextual decision. A bandit selects among a fixed, pre-registered menu of feasible heuristic policies or a small bounded adjustment to their rank. The learned component cannot unmask infeasible actions or override deadline/service gates. The experiment must show value over the best fixed heuristic out of sample, not merely over FCFS.
5. **Revisit MARL only for a changed research question.** If separate regional controllers are required, model real local authority and inter-region coordination explicitly (e.g. regional admission proposals plus destination accept/deny/capacity response, or simultaneous per-region queues and actions). Then benchmark IQL and VDN/QMIX or MAPPO with the true global state and a shared constrained objective. This would be a new formulation, not a drop-in algorithm swap.

The recommendation is conditional on the objective being carbon-efficient execution with high completion and SLA compliance. A more sophisticated method is not itself a contribution; a defensible contribution requires a harder, realistic sequential decision and a clear gap over strong non-learning methods.

## Proposed architecture (for a future phase; not implemented)

```text
Trace / forecast input
        ↓
State builder: task slack + candidate delta carbon/energy + transfer/runtime
               + active reservations/queue + forecast uncertainty/horizon
        ↓
Hard feasibility and service gate (GPU/CPU/memory/deadline; never learned)
        ↓
Fixed heuristic menu: greedy carbon | EDF+carbon | least-slack+carbon | defer-window
        ↓
Optional contextual bandit selects/ranks only feasible heuristic outputs
        ↓
Deterministic reservation/conflict commit → simulator → full service/carbon feedback
```

For oracle evaluation, add a parallel offline optimizer for small finite workloads. The dispatcher policy remains centralized initially because it matches the simulator's actual authority. If later distributing authority, define each region's local observation, proposal/acceptance protocol, shared objective, inactive-agent semantics and conflict resolution before selecting a MARL algorithm.

## Experimental methodology

### Stage A — Validate the simulator's optimization target

- Freeze energy, idle-power sharing, PUE, migration, execution-speed, carbon-slot integration and deadline semantics. Add independent hand-computed cases and a model audit for incremental candidate costs versus final integrated totals.
- Establish a transparent carbon objective: total incremental operational CO2e subject to declared completion and SLA constraints. Report rejection as a service failure, never as a carbon saving. State whether waiting consumes energy and whether regional idle energy is in scope.
- Generate paired workloads across load levels (light, near saturation, overloaded), arrival burstiness, task sizes/deadline slack, migration dataset sizes, and carbon traces with realistic temporal variation and forecast error. Keep generator families and seeds split into train, validation, and final held-out sets. Synthetic tests should supplement, not stand in for, properly licensed small trace samples.
- Compare FCFS, fastest/least-latency, EDF, least slack, current greedy, EDF/least-slack plus carbon, and a small-instance offline oracle. Use identical tasks, carbon profiles, forecasts and resource constraints. Report mean, SD and paired/crossed 95% intervals by workload family; preserve every run-level output.
- Record candidate-level regret (selected delta-carbon minus best feasible delta-carbon), oracle optimality gap, service metrics and runtime. This tells whether gains are possible and where the heuristic makes mistakes.

### Stage B — Test whether temporal decisions matter

- Use ablations that remove future carbon forecasts, capacity contention, migration cost, regional execution heterogeneity, and arrivals after the current task. Keep one factor changed per ablation.
- Count decisions where current greedy and one-step/rolling-horizon oracle disagree and classify why: future congestion, future carbon window, deadline shadow price, shared idle overhead or model error.
- If disagreements and attainable improvements are rare, stop learning experiments. If frequent, identify the feature/transition that explains each and ensure the state contains it before training.

### Stage C — Evaluate hybrid learning only after Stage A/B

- Freeze the heuristic menu and bandit context/reward before final evaluation. Use matched train/validation workloads to select hyperparameters; keep final held-out traces unopened until final scoring.
- Compare fixed best heuristic, current greedy, contextual bandit heuristic selector, and an oracle upper bound. Optionally include a centralized single-agent Q-learning/RL control only if Stage B demonstrates sequential state value. Do not compare against strawman FCFS alone.
- Use at least 5 independent training seeds and a substantially larger held-out workload set spanning each workload family; report paired differences with service metrics. Include compute/sample budget, action distribution, calibration, learning curves and ablation of bandit context/reward. Provide an online regret analysis where its assumptions apply.
- Predeclare a go/no-go rule: the learned policy must improve carbon against the best fixed heuristic with a paired 95% interval excluding zero while meeting non-inferiority thresholds for completion and SLA, across workload families. If not, retain the heuristic.

## Implementation roadmap (planning only)

1. **Assessment and freeze (now):** retain all current source/results and use this report as the rationale for no new model training.
2. **Simulator/objective audit:** verify marginal-vs-total carbon calculations, shared overhead, queues, active resources, deferral, deadlines, task completion and rejection counting; document the precise objective.
3. **Baseline and oracle protocol:** define EDF/least-slack/carbon variants and a small-instance solver or exhaustive oracle; test costs and feasibility independently.
4. **Workload/trace validation:** add multiple controlled synthetic families and, after license/schema checks, a small reproducible trace subset with realistic carbon time series and forecast error.
5. **Sequential-value ablations:** quantify when lookahead changes decisions and what state is needed; decide whether a contextual bandit is sufficient.
6. **Hybrid bandit pilot (conditional):** implement only if a measurable heuristic/oracle gap and repeatable sequential effect remain; use the safety-gated architecture and preregistered evaluation.
7. **MARL reconsideration (conditional, separate formulation):** proceed only if the study introduces actual simultaneous decentralized regional decisions and shows why a centralized policy is unsuitable; then compare Q-based factorization and MAPPO under the redesigned contract.
